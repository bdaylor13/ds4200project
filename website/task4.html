TASK 4

What's the relationship between minimum nights and occupancy?

Design: This stacked histogram displays the distribution of "Minimum Nights" settings, specifically zoomed in on short-term requirements (1-15 nights). The color represents the availability level over the last 365 days, categorized into quartiles based on the data distribution. Lighter green indicates high availability (top 25%, often professional listings) and dark blue indicates low availability (bottom 25%).

Takeaways:

  • The "1-2 Night" Standard: The overwhelming majority of listings set their minimum stay to just 1 or 2 nights. This suggests that most hosts prioritize flexibility to capture maximum demand from weekenders and short-term tourists.
  • Avoidance of the "Middle Ground": There is a significant drop-off in listings requiring 4 to 14 nights. This duration is often too long for tourists yet too short for temporary residents, making it a less attractive strategy for hosts.

The impact of regulations on long-term stays...

Design: Following the same visual structure, this chart shifts focus to listings with longer minimum stay requirements (28 to 60 nights), skipping the negligible 16-27 night range and excluding extreme outliers. It highlights how regulatory definitions and monthly rental norms influence host behavior, distinct from the short-term market.

Takeaways:

  • The "30-Night" Regulatory Wall:
    A massive spike is visible exactly at the 30-night mark. This is a direct response to Boston's short-term rental regulations. By requiring a minimum of 30 days, hosts can bypass strict "under 28 days" rules, taxes, and registration requirements, effectively operating as monthly rentals instead.
  • Polarized Professionalism: The "Very High" availability listings (light green) are clustered at the extremes: either 1-2 nights or 30+ nights. Investors and professional hosts tend to choose either full compliance for short-term stays or the regulatory safety of monthly leases, avoiding the complex zone in between.
