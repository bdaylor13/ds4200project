Decoding Airbnb Success in Boston

-- What Makes a Listing Successful? --

Introduction

The rise of short term rentals in cities across the nation has created both challenges for policy makers as well as economic opportunities for property owners. Despite a 2019 agreement between Boston and Airbnb requiring all listings to display registration numbers, recent investigations reveal ongoing compliance gaps. With nightly rates in the Boston area increasing over the years and thousands of active listings, understanding what drives success for short term rentals has become essential for market participants. Boston is known to be a major tourist city with various neighborhoods that attract many different crowds. Thus, it is important to understand the factors that determine listing performance, aside from relying on intuition or ambiguous data. For our project, we decided to dive into AirBnb's impact in Boston, specifically analyzing the success of Airbnb's in various neighborhoods. Through our analysis, we hope to provide insights for Boston policy makers and hosts, uncovering market patterns and competitive dynamics that may not be immediately obvious. We hope to highlight what factors contribute to listing success as well as the relationship between pricing strategies and occupancy. Our visualizations will aim to turn this complex dataset into communicable insights by finding patterns and trends that are not obvious from the raw numbers alone.

Our Data

Our data comes from Inside Airbnb which houses Airbnb listing data for different cities. We decided it would be fitting to look at listings in the Boston area. On the site for each city there are many datasets to choose from, like the neighborhoods or reviews datasets. We settled on the listings dataset because in the description, the site said it was good for visualizations, a key part of this project. The dataset has 18 columns and 4,561 rows, with a good mix of quantitative and qualitative columns that we used to form our visualizations.

References

Challenges of Illegal Units

This article published by the Mayor's Office in 2019 highlights the persistent issue of regulatory challenges for Boston policy makers regarding Airbnb licenses. The mayor of Boston at the time, Martin J. Walsh, signed an agreement with Airbnb to ensure that illegal short-term rentals were no longer displayed on the website. The agreement requires that any Airbnb property should be registered with the city of Boston prior to being posted on the website. This was in an effort to preserve affordable housing in Boston as well as halt the growth of private monopolies in Boston's short-term rental housing market.

Underground Airbnb Network

This article reinforces part of the motivation behind our analysis as policy makers continue to struggle to enforce regulations in 2025, with many listings operating under invalid permits. Crudele writes that the city of Boston has only issued 1,160 short term rental permits. However, networks mentioned in the article, such as “AirbnbQueen”, are operating many listings without licenses. As our dataset includes all Airbnb listings, regardless of license status, we are hoping to capture the concerns described in this article.
