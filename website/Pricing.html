Pricing

What pricing strategies lead to higher booking rates?

Exploring the relationship between pricing and revenue across neighborhoods...

Interaction: Hover over points to see information on each individual neighborhood.

Takeaways:

  • The $275-300 range acheives highest booking rates: The largest points indicate the highest occupancies, indicating that this range of pricing seems to be the high-demand price point where listings may be more likely to stay booked.
  • The $150-250 ranges seems to be a "safe zone": The listings in this range seem to achieve consistent booking rates, with bubbles in this range being medium to large in size.
  • Very high pricing ($350 +) seems to limit bookings: The smaller two right-most bubbles indicate that neighborhoods with very high pricing may lead to reduced occupancy rates.

Looking at the same relationship within the neighborhoods...

Interaction: Hover over points to see information on each individual listing. Use the dropdown to select different neighborhoods.

Takeaways:

  • High occupancy drives high revenue: The darkest points (highest occupancy rates) consistently appear at the top of the chart (highest revenue), indicating that maximizing booking rates is key to maximizing revenue regardless of price point.
  • Lower prices -> higher booking rates: The darker colors on the left hand side of the visuals for most neighborhoods indicate that listings with lower prices tend to achieve higher occupancy rates.
  • Higher prices -> lower booking rates: As you move right across the plot, the points get lighter, indicating that premium pricing significantly reduces occupancy in many cases.
  • A tradeoff exists: While there seems to be a clear inverse relationship between pricing and occupancy, pricing and revenue seem to have a positive relationship. That is, there is a tradeoff that hosts must face when it comes to pricing. They must choose between wanting to mazimize occupancy versus maximizing revenue per booking.
